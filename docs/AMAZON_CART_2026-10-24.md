## Portable setup (picked 2026-10-25)

This setup is built for carrying to a lab or to friends with the least fuss. **The whole gripper runs from one USB-C cable into a power bank**, and no laptop is needed for a demo.

- **Power bank:** any USB-C power bank that says "PD" and 20 W, about 10,000 mAh. Your phone power bank probably works. It is the battery and its own charger.
- **Trigger board set to 9 V**, not 12 V. Every PD power bank offers 9 V, and so does any USB-C laptop or phone charger of 18 W and up. So if the bank is flat, plug into the professor's laptop charger and keep going.
- **7805 regulator + diode** (fablab parts, see below): powers the Pico from the same 9 V. A laptop can still be plugged in at the same time for ROS 2.
- **Demo mode:** with no laptop attached, the Pico's own BOOTSEL button opens and closes the jaws. This goes into the firmware; no extra button is needed.
- **What 9 V costs you:** top speed. It is capped at 300 rpm, which is 40 mm/s, or 0.8 s for a full open-to-close stroke. Grip force is unchanged, because it depends on motor current, not voltage.

**Before the first power-up:** set the trigger board to 9 V. Some boards use a button, some a solder jumper. Check with a multimeter that its output reads 9 V before connecting it to the driver.

## Notes

1. **Dowels 4 × 40.** Amazon US has no hardened 4 × 36. Press them 6.5 mm into the base as modelled; they stand 4.5 mm above the cover and are clear of the controller board (checked in the model).
2. **Shoulder screws.** These are 304 stainless. The shear safety factor at the 200 N limit is 12, which is plenty.
3. **Pico clone.** It runs the same MicroPython firmware. Before soldering, check the pin labels match the Raspberry Pi Pico layout.
