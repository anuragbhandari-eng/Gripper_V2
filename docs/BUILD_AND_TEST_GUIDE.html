REV 7.2b · BUILD AND TEST GUIDE

From a box of parts to a working gripper

Nine parts, A to I. Every step ends with a check, so a problem shows up where it starts. Tick the boxes as you go: this page remembers them on this device.

If a check fails, stop. Copy what the Thonny shell printed, or describe what you see, and ask Claude before going on.

blue in a picture = parts added in that step pale = already fitted

Five safety rules

  1. Never plug or unplug the motor or the driver while the power is on. That kills the TMC2209 instantly. Unplug the 9 V and the laptop USB first.
  2. The driver current is a safety setting. Set VREF to 0.99 V before the motor is ever connected, and never higher. At full current the motor can push about 259 N, far more than the printed parts are designed for.
  3. Use 9 V only. Set the trigger board to 9 V and measure it before it touches the board.
  4. Tighten every M3 screw only until snug, with the short arm of the key (0.15 N·m). More than that crushes the ABS.
  5. Keep fingers out of the jaws while it's powered. With the driver off the jaws go limp and can be pushed by hand. That is normal: the screw back-drives.

What you need besides the parts

Tools

  • Soldering iron, with a spare or conical tip for heat-set inserts
  • Multimeter and calipers
  • Metric hex keys: 2 mm for every M3 screw, 5 mm for M6, 3 or 4 mm for the M5 screws (depends on the kit), plus a 2 mm ball-end
  • 4.0 mm drill bit (turned by hand) and a 3.2 mm drill bit
  • Dremel with an abrasive cut-off wheel. The dowels are hardened; a hacksaw won't cut them
  • Hacksaw for the lead screw, small file
  • Bench vise with soft jaws, flush cutters, pliers
  • Small plastic or ceramic screwdriver for the driver's pot
  • Safety glasses for cutting and pressing steel
  • Optional: M5 tap

Consumables

  • Solder, heat-shrink, tape
  • Hookup wire: thin (24 AWG) for signals, thicker (20 AWG) for 9 V and the motor
  • 30-minute epoxy and CA (super) glue
  • Light grease, and a little wax or grease as a release agent
  • Zip ties
  • 2 M8 hex nuts
  • USB-C cable from the laptop to the Pico
  • USB-C cable from the power bank to the trigger board
  • 20 W USB-C PD power bank

A3

Magnet test

DONE

Do this before printing the base and cover. The magnet sits inside the steel 608, which can weaken its field. This 5-minute test confirms the encoder still reads well there.

  1. Install MicroPython on the Pico (step C1).
  2. Glue the magnet into the puck from the fit test, and push the puck into a 608.
  3. Wire the AS5600 to the Pico: VCC → 3V3 (pin 36), GND → GND (pin 38), SDA → GP0 (pin 1), SCL → GP1 (pin 2), DIR → GND.
  4. Hold the board flat in front of the magnet, chip centred on it and about 0.8 mm away. The test only means something in that position.
  5. Open as5600_bench_test.py in Thonny, run it, and turn the 608's inner ring slowly by hand.
Check

Stop if it says "too weak" or AGC sits at 128, and tell Claude. The magnet would then have to move in front of the bearing, which needs a longer block.

C

Electronics on the bench

Do this while the parts print.

C1

Install MicroPython and Thonny

DONE
  1. Install Thonny on your laptop.
  2. Download the latest MicroPython UF2 for the Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) from micropython.org.
  3. Hold the Pico's BOOTSEL button while plugging it into the laptop. Drag the UF2 onto the RPI-RP2 drive that appears.
  4. In Thonny: Tools → Options → Interpreter → "MicroPython (Raspberry Pi Pico)".
  5. Copy bench.py to the Pico: open it in Thonny, then File → Save as → Raspberry Pi Pico → bench.py.
Check
C2

Solder the controller board

DONE
  1. Cut the 50 × 70 mm protoboard down to 48 × 60 mm: score along a row of holes and snap it, or saw it.
  2. Drill 4 mounting holes to 3.2 mm, in two rows. Each row is 3 mm in from a short (48 mm) edge, so the rows are 54 mm apart. In each row:
    • one hole is 3 mm from the long edge on the driver side;
    • the other is 36.5 mm from that same edge, on the Pico's centreline between its two socket rows.
  3. Cut the 1 × 40 socket strips into two 1 × 20 rows for the Pico and two 1 × 8 rows for the driver. Check which way round the driver goes (EN pin to EN) before soldering its sockets.
  4. Lay the parts out as in the picture, so the cover fits:
    • Pico sockets along one long edge, USB end at a short edge.
    • Driver sockets beside the Pico, toward the other short edge.
    • Trigger board taped flat at the USB end next to the Pico, its socket just over the edge.
    • 7805 lying flat beside it, legs bent down 90°, tab 6–7 mm in from the edge and clear of the corner screw.
    • 100 µF capacitor standing between the 7805 and the driver.
  5. Wire it to the table below. Keep the 9 V wires short and thick.

Power check, boards out

DONE
  1. Set the trigger board to 9 V (a button on some boards, a solder jumper on others). Plug it into the power bank and measure 9.0 V ± 0.3 at its output before it touches the board.
  2. Connect it to the board with neither the Pico nor the driver plugged in, and measure the four points below.
  3. Unplug the power.
Check with the multimeter

C5

Driver in: set the current, then test 3

DONE
  1. Unplug the power. Plug the driver into its socket. The motor stays disconnected.
  2. Plug in the 9 V. Measure between the small pot on the driver and GND, and turn the pot with a plastic or ceramic screwdriver until it reads 0.99 V. That sets a 0.70 A ceiling on the BTT TMC2209 V1.3. On a different driver board, ask Claude for its value first.
  3. In Thonny, run the test.

Check
C6

Make the motor cable

DONE

The motor has 4 wires in 2 coil pairs. Find the pairs with the multimeter on ohms: wires of the same pair read about 2.2 Ω, wires from different pairs read open.

  • One pair goes to the driver's first coil pins (1A/1B), the other to the second (2A/2B).
  • If the motor later turns the wrong way, the software fixes it in test F4. There's no need to swap wires.

Don't connect the motor to the board yet.

Check

D3

Locating pins and dowels

DONE
  1. Locating pins: screw two M3×16 screws into the bearing-block half of the base until 8 mm of thread is in. Then cut the heads off and deburr the ends.
  2. Dowels: cut each 4 × 40 dowel to 38.5 mm with the abrasive cut-off disc. Deburr the cut end.
  3. Press them up through the base from underneath at O1 and O2 on both sides, square to the plate, until flush with the underside. Use the vise with soft jaws, and put a tube or drilled block over the dowel on the top side: about 29.5 mm comes out there.
  4. Smear wax or grease on the top 8 mm of each dowel, so the epoxy in D10 doesn't stick to it.

Don't fit the bronze bushings yet. The cranks have to go over the dowels first. The bushings go on in D10.

Check
D4

Prepare the lead screw

DONE
  1. Collar pull test: tighten the lock collar's set screw on the end of the screw and pull hard by hand. It must not slip. Then take it off.
  2. Cut the screw to 73.0 mm from the motor's mounting face (71.0 mm past the round pilot), tolerance −0.5 / +0. Never long: the encoder puck fills the last 3 mm.
  3. File a small chamfer on the cut end, clean off the chips and grease the thread.
Check
D5

Nut, carriage, collar and bearing onto the screw

DONE
  1. Slide the brass nut into the carriage from the front: body into the bore, flange into the front recess, holes at 3 and 9 o'clock. Fit 2× M3×8, snug only.
  2. Spin the carriage onto the screw.
  3. Slide on the lock collar with its set screw loose, then the 608.
Check
D6

Drive into the base

DONE
  1. Lay it all into the base with the motor connector facing up:
    • the pilot goes in the wall recess;
    • the carriage tongue goes in the channel;
    • the 608 sits in its cradle against the front lip, collar behind it.
  2. Fit the 2 lower motor screws (M3×8) through the access slots with the 2 mm ball-end.
  3. Push the collar forward until it just touches the 608 inner ring, with no preload, and tighten its set screw.
  4. Turn the motor shaft by hand. The carriage must run end to end without binding. Then tighten the 2 nut screws.
Check
D7

Encoder

DONE
  1. Glue the magnet into the puck, flush. Push the puck into the 608 bore against the screw end and fix it there with a drop of CA.
  2. Solder 4 wires (VCC, GND, SDA, SCL) to the AS5600 pad row, plus a short link from DIR to GND.
  3. Drop the board into the slot in front of the 608, chip toward the magnet and pads up.
Check
D8

Linkage, both sides

DONE

Parts marked U and L are mirror images, one for each side. If a part doesn't sit flat with its boss up, it belongs on the other side.

  1. BellCrank Lower on O1 and FrontCrank Lower on O2, bosses up.
  2. Finger onto F1 and F2. Conrod onto D, and between the carriage ears at C.
  3. BellCrank Upper and FrontCrank Upper on top.
  4. Shoulder screws at F1, F2, D and C, into the inserts. Snug only.
Check
D9

Tip blocks and pads

DONE

Fit the narrow set first.

  1. Slide a TPU pad into a tip block's groove from the rear and press until its barb snaps past the step. A drop of soapy water helps.
  2. Push the tip block up onto the finger tongue from below, and fit 2× M3×10 from below.
Check
D10

Cover and bushings, then wait overnight

DONE
  1. Thread the encoder wires up through the slit in the cover as you lower it, and check the motor connector is free.
  2. Put a little epoxy into the 4 oversize dowel holes in the cover, and lower the cover over the dowels.
  3. Push a bronze bushing down over each dowel top into its hole, until the bushing's top is about 1.4 mm below the cover top. Wipe any epoxy off the dowel tops.
  4. Fit the cover screws: 2× M3×20 at the motor wall, 2× M3×20 at the bearing-block ears, 2× M3×8 at the posts, then the 2 upper motor screws (M3×8) through the cover slots.
  5. Let it cure fully, overnight, with everything assembled. The bushings bond exactly where the dowels are.

A bushing must not rest on the crank underneath. Look again after 10 minutes and lift any that has slid down.

Check the next day, power off

Once the controller is on, two cover screws sit under its board. To take the cover off later, remove the controller first.

E

Controller onto the gripper

E1

Mount the board and its cover

DONE
  1. Unplug the Pico and the driver from their sockets. Put the 4 printed spacers (4 mm) on the cover-top inserts, set the board on them, and fit 4× M3×10 through the board and spacers. Then plug the Pico and driver back in.
  2. Power off: connect the motor cable to the driver and the AS5600 lead to the board.
  3. Zip-tie each cable (USB, power, motor, encoder) to the board as strain relief.
  4. Lower the electronics cover over the board with the cables in its notches:
    • both USB-C cables through the two notches in the short wall at the USB end;
    • motor cable at the motor end;
    • encoder wires at the jaw end.
  5. Fit 2× M3×8 through its flanges. Do this before plugging in the Pico's USB cable: the plug sits over one of the screws.
Check

G

Bench stand and desk clamps

G1

Build the stand

DONE
  1. Slide the T-nuts into the column's slots first: 2 near the bottom for the base socket and 2 for the head bracket.
  2. Push the column into the base socket and fit 2× M5×12 through the socket walls into the T-nuts. Optional: M5×12 from below into the column end, if you tapped it M5.
  3. Drop an M8 nut into the slot of each desk clamp and thread a star knob in from below. Seat a TPU clamp pad on the knob's screw tip, socket side down.
  4. Put the base on the desk with its front edge flush with the desk's front edge. Hook a clamp over each front corner (its boss drops into the corner counterbore) and tighten the knobs by hand.
  5. Slide the head bracket onto the column and lock it with 2× M5×12 into its T-nuts. Drop 4 M6 nuts into the bracket pockets from the back.
  6. Bolt the adapter to the bracket with 4× M6×20. Slide the gripper onto the adapter (the U-slot is open at the rear) and fit 4× M3×8 through the side walls.

Always clamp it. The stand tips over if it isn't clamped to the desk.

Check
H

Demo mode and taking it with you

H1

Stand-alone demo, no laptop

DONE

Save main.py on the Pico next to bench.py. From then on, plugging the USB-C cable into a power bank makes the gripper home itself and open. Each press of the Pico's BOOTSEL button then grips or releases. To stop the demo, connect Thonny, or delete main.py from the Pico.

Check

A power bank that switches itself off: some banks turn off when little current is drawn. The driver's hold current normally keeps the bank awake. If yours still switches off, press its button, or use another bank or a wall charger.
